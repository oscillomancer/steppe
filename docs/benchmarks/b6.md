# B6: what a bridged plug-in call costs

**Baseline (2026-11-14, before live bridging; ADR-0013 synchronous bridge,
revision 984f2d5e plus the bench tool):** Core Ultra 5 225F, 10 CPUs,
`powersave` governor, kernel 7.3.4-zen2, a desktop session running, Release
build (`cmake --preset release`). `bridge-bench` runs the hermetic gain
fixture (`com.suppleaudio.test.gain`, effectively no DSP), so these are the
cost of the call itself: shared memory, wake, the bridge thread's turn and
the answer. A cycle is paced to its period on the steady clock, as a device
callback would be, and processes every instance in turn on the calling
thread, a chain of `n` bridged stages. 1000 measured cycles after 200
warm-up cycles, one run per row, so p99 rests on about ten cycles and the
maximum on one; times in µs, 48 kHz.

```sh
build/release/host/clap-host/bridge-bench --cycles 1000 \
  [--caller-priority 80 --bridge-priority 79|10] --output b6.json
```

What it shows:

- With the bridge thread in the live class, a bridged stage costs about
  10 µs more than in-process at the median at 64 frames (11 against 2 µs)
  and about 20 µs more at 1024 (34 against 15). Its p99 ranges from 14 µs
  to 337 µs across rows: the wake of a thread whose core went idle under
  `powersave`.
- In today's background class (FIFO 10) under a FIFO 80 caller, a single
  shared-bridge stage took 134 to 202 µs at the median, while a dedicated
  one took 11 to 33 µs; I have not found why the shared process differs.
  ADR-0081 point 5 raises the class while live; with it, both are 11 to
  38 µs.
- No row in the live class ran a cycle past its period, up to 32 stages at
  64 frames (p99 434 µs shared, 553 µs dedicated, against 1333 µs). Rows of
  32 stages did in the other setups: up to 11 of 1000 cycles in the
  background class, up to 23 with normal scheduling.
- In-process stages chain far more cheaply: 32 stages at 64 frames cost
  29 µs at p99 against 434 µs bridged. Serial bridged chains are the cost to
  watch at small buffers.

These are one machine's numbers with a no-op plug-in; they do not include a
plug-in's own DSP, and macOS and Windows are unmeasured (native CI runs on
virtual machines; device trials are opt-in). The live-path measurements of
ADR-0081 follow when it is implemented.
